# man03_mosaic_tile - Hand-set mosaic tile

_Category: ceramic. Open the graph: `cookbook/ceramic/man03_mosaic_tile.ptex`._

A warm ivory tile mosaic with a near-black warm grout line, laid in a
running-bond course with visible per-tile jitter rather than a perfect
grid. The first cookbook material to use `skewed_bricks`, and this
category's second material after `man02_ceramic_hex_tiles`.

## Recipe

`skewed_bricks` is a standalone brick-tile generator: all three of its
inputs (`mortar_map`/`bevel_map`/`round_map`) are optional `f` maps with
function defaults, so it works without a donor and without any of them
wired up. (The plan originally targeted `custom_tiles` for this slot,
but that node requires an `sdf2d`-typed shape input which only the
out-of-scope SDF family can produce, so it was swapped for
`skewed_bricks` during authoring.) Built from scratch via
`_from_scratch_noise_material` (no donor has this topology), then the
placeholder generator is `retype()`d to `skewed_bricks` at its own
catalog defaults: `rows=6, columns=3, offset=0.5, randomness=1,
mortar=0.1, bevel=0.1, round=0, corner=0.3`. `skewed_bricks`' port 0
("Bricks Pattern", a plain `f` grayscale) matches `perlin`'s output, so
the swap is connection-safe.

**The key differentiator from `man02_ceramic_hex_tiles`.** `man02` is a
perfectly regular hex grid built from `beehive`, with zero per-cell
jitter. `skewed_bricks`' `randomness=1` offsets each brick's vertical cut
from a perfect grid line, so courses read as hand-set or reclaimed
rather than machine-uniform. An isolated verification render of the
retyped node (un-recolored, raw 0..1 scalar as grayscale) confirmed a
clean rectangular running-bond grid (6 rows / 3 columns, staggered by
`offset=0.5`) with each vertical mortar seam visibly skewed course to
course, plainly distinct in kind from `man02`'s dead-straight hex edges.
Each brick also shows a soft diagonal bevel facet from `bevel=0.1`,
giving individual bricks a slight embossed look.

**Gradient stops are histogram-matched.** A measured 96x96-sample
histogram found 32.5% of samples exactly 0 (deep mortar interior,
already a ~33% plateau by position 0.10) and 50% exactly 1.0 (deep
tile-face interior), with the climb from mortar to majority-face
happening across roughly positions 0.12-0.30. The albedo and roughness
ramps both hold flat through 0.12 (the measured mortar plateau) and
finish their transition by 0.30 (past which the signal is overwhelmingly
face pixels), rather than spreading evenly across the full 0..1 range.

Palette is a warm ivory tile face (deliberately not `man02`'s stark
white, to keep the two hero ceramics visually distinct) with a
near-black warm grout line.

**Roughness deviates from the standing "flat texture" recipe on
purpose.** Like `man02`'s own face-vs-grout technique, `rough_const` is
wired from the same retyped field as the albedo rather than a flat
two-point same-value gradient, with its gradient stops at the identical
positions as the albedo ramp but inverted (high roughness on the grout,
low/glazed roughness on the tile face) -- a flat scalar would light the
recessed grout and the glazed face identically, losing the ceramic
contrast this material is built to show. `normal_map`'s `param4=0` is
the standing flat-normal fix.

## Subgraph structure

Two named subgraphs:

- **Mosaic Pattern** -- the retyped `skewed_bricks` generator and its
  albedo colorize. Exposed: `Rows`, `Jitter` (`randomness`), `Tile and
  grout color`.
- **Mosaic Finish** -- the normal map and the face/grout roughness
  texture. Exposed: `Grout depth` (the normal map's relief strength),
  `Roughness`.

## Honest limitation

This recipe's roughness inversion mirrors `man02`'s own technique rather
than introducing a new one, so the two ceramic materials share a
face-vs-grout contrast mechanism even though their underlying pattern
generators (`beehive` vs `skewed_bricks`) are unrelated -- a deliberate
reuse of a proven technique, not an oversight.
