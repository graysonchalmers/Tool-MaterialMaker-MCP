# t10_packed_dirt - Packed dirt

_Category: terrain. Open the graph: `cookbook/terrain/t10_packed_dirt.ptex`._

Muted brown/tan packed earth with irregular, soft-edged merged blotches
of grime. The first cookbook material to use `dirt`, a compound node
with three internal composite sub-networks, and a genuinely new terrain
topology this category has never shipped before.

## Recipe

Built from scratch via `_from_scratch_noise_material` (no donor has this
topology), then the placeholder generator is `retype()`d to `dirt` --
the same connection-safe swap `t07_forest_floor`/`t09_rippled_wet_sand`
already use, since a compound node's port 0 is a plain `f` output like
`perlin`'s.

**Chosen mode found by comparing all three, not by trusting the first.**
`dirt` has three internal sub-networks (`param0` = 0/1/2, "Dirt
1"/"Dirt 2"/"Dirt 3"), each blending a hexagonal `shape` field with
`fbm2` noise through a tiler. Isolated verification renders of all
three, at the node's own defaults (`d_scale=1, param1=11`), found:

- `param0=0` ("Dirt 1"): a dense scatter of small, mostly circular,
  individually distinct soft-edged dots -- reads as granular
  speckle/spatter, not merged patches.
- `param0=1` ("Dirt 2"): much finer, closer to uniform static/grain,
  almost no larger-scale clustering.
- `param0=2` ("Dirt 3", the one shipped here): visibly larger,
  elongated, irregular blotches with soft feathered edges that merge
  into loose connected patches -- the only one of the three that reads
  as "irregular soft-edged blotchy patches" rather than a dot-scatter or
  fine grain.

None of the three modes show a visible hexagonal grid at these settings
(the brief's anticipated risk did not materialize), but they are not
equivalent to each other, and mode 2 is the clear match for this
material's target look. `d_scale` only makes the pattern finer as it
increases, so `d_scale=1` is already the largest-patch setting
available.

**Gradient stops are histogram-matched, not evenly spaced.** A measured
grayscale histogram of the chosen mode's render came back mean 0.22,
median 0.20, p95 0.49, p99 0.63, max 1.0 -- the field sits mostly in the
bottom third of its range with only a thin tail reaching higher values.
The albedo gradient below is remapped so its stops sit at the measured
percentiles (dark tone at the median, mid tan at p95, light highlight
only near the true max) rather than at naive 0.0/0.5/1.0 positions,
which would have collapsed the material to near-uniform dark and never
shown its documented tan highlight.

High matte roughness (0.88) -- the deliberate opposite of
`t09_rippled_wet_sand`'s 0.15 wet sheen in the same file -- fed as a
flat texture so an ORM map exports. `normal_map param1=0.3` for a
moderate, worn unevenness -- `dirt` has no crack topology to begin with,
so this relief reads as rolling, worn ground rather than sharp fissures.
`param4=0` is the standing flat-normal fix.

## Subgraph structure

Two named subgraphs:

- **Dirt Pattern** -- the retyped `dirt` generator and its albedo
  colorize. Exposed: `Dirt color`, `Grain scale` (`d_scale`).
- **Dirt Finish** -- the normal map and the flat roughness texture.
  Exposed: `Roughness`, `Surface relief`.

## Honest limitation

This is a topology this category has never had before (irregular
blotchy patches, distinct from the connected-crack-network,
broad-isotropic-roll, and anisotropic-banding families already in this
file), but `dirt`'s three internal modes are a fixed menu -- there was
no way to dial in, say, a coarser or finer blotch size independent of
picking a mode, since `d_scale` only tightens the pattern rather than
offering a genuinely different scale range.
