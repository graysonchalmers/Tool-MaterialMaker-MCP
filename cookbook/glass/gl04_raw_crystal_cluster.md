# gl04_raw_crystal_cluster - Raw crystal cluster

_Category: glass. Open the graph: `cookbook/glass/gl04_raw_crystal_cluster.ptex`._

A jewel-toned amethyst crystal cluster: irregular, variably-sized
polygonal cells with bright pointed glints near cell centers and dark
crack-like valleys at cell boundaries, like a raw geode or quartz
cluster rather than a cut or fractured surface. The first cookbook
material to use `crystal`, a fourth glass topology distinct from all
three existing siblings.

## Recipe

Built from scratch via `_from_scratch_noise_material` (no donor has this
topology), then the placeholder generator is `retype()`d to `crystal` at
its own defaults: `param0=16, param1=16` (Scale X/Scale Y, read from
`crystal.mmg`'s own `gen_parameters` block, which drives two
independently-seeded internal `voronoi` fields combined via math into
one crystalline cell pattern). Both node types expose a plain `f` scalar
on output port 0, so the swap is connection-safe.

**How this differs from its glass siblings.** `gl01_frosted_glass` is a
connected sandblast crack network off plain `voronoi` (soft, diffuse,
matte). `gl02_cut_gem` is faceted `voronoi_triangle` cells (uniform,
flat, hard-edged, no cracks). `gl03_shattered_crystal` is a dense
continuous `shard_fbm` fracture field (hard-edged, covers the whole
surface). `crystal`'s two-voronoi composite is none of those.

**Verified, not assumed.** An isolated verification render of the
retyped node confirmed the raw grayscale output is an irregular mosaic
of polygonal cells, clearly varying in size and shape (some cells span
several times the area of their neighbors), separated by dark
crack-like boundaries, with a sharp bright pointed highlight near the
center of many (not all) cells -- visibly distinct from `gl02`'s uniform
`voronoi_triangle` mosaic.

**Gradient stops are histogram-matched.** The verification render's
pixel histogram came back heavily skewed dark: median 23/255 (~0.09
normalized), p90 67 (~0.26), p99 117 (~0.46), max 223 (~0.87) -- roughly
half the field sits below 0.09 and the bright glints are a thin tail, not
a flat 0..1 spread. `CrystalColor`'s gradient stops are placed against
that measured distribution: 0.0 and 0.08 bracket the dense low end with
the deepest valley tones, 0.22 and 0.45 cover the p75-p99 range with
mid-facet tones, and 0.80 catches the sparse bright tail with a
near-white glint color -- so the crack-valley/facet/glint structure
actually survives into the final render instead of collapsing into one
flat dark hue.

Palette is jewel-tone amethyst purple (deep violet valleys through
lavender mid-tones to near-white glints), distinct from `gl02`'s flat
emerald and `gl03`'s blue-violet. Roughness is pushed low (0.06, at
`gl02_cut_gem`'s glossy calibration point) for actually-glossy crystal
faces. `normal_map`'s `param1=0.7` sits between `gl02`'s 0.6 and `gl03`'s
0.8, for sharp crystalline facet relief without over-exaggerating into
`gl03`'s shattered look. `param4=0` is the standing flat-normal fix.

## Subgraph structure

Two named subgraphs:

- **Crystal Pattern** -- the retyped `crystal` generator and its albedo
  colorize. Exposed: `Cell scale` (`param0`), `Crystal color`.
- **Crystal Finish** -- the normal map and the flat roughness texture.
  Exposed: `Roughness`, `Surface relief`.

## Honest limitation

Like every material in this category, Material Maker's `material` node
has no true refraction, dispersion, or internal light-transport model,
so this recipe cannot simulate how light actually bends inside a real
crystal. It approximates the look (an irregular natural crystal-cluster
surface, glossy jewel-tone finish, sharp facet relief) as an opaque,
physically-shaded surface -- appropriate for how a tileable texture like
this gets used, not a claim about real crystal optics.
