# m04_scratched_steel - Scratched steel

_Category: metal. Open the graph: `cookbook/metal/m04_scratched_steel.ptex`._

A cool gray steel panel scored with discrete, individually-angled, wavy
scratch marks composed in layers. The first cookbook material to use the
`scratches` node, proving out a genuinely different technique from this
category's other two brushed metals.

## Recipe

Built from scratch via `_from_scratch_noise_material` (no existing donor
has this topology), then the placeholder generator is `retype()`d to
`scratches` using the node's own verified catalog defaults: `length=0.25,
width=0.5, layers=4, waviness=0.5, angle=0, randomness=0.5`. Both the
placeholder and `scratches` expose a plain `f` scalar on output port 0, so
the swap is connection-safe.

**How this differs from its metal siblings.** `m02_brushed_aluminum` and
`m03_brushed_titanium` both read as continuous, uniform brushed finishes
(a stretched `perlin` and `noise_anisotropic` respectively). `scratches`
composes discrete, individually-angled, wavy scratch marks in layers
instead, so this material reads as scuffed and scored rather than
brushed. Palette is cooler and less uniform than either sibling (a
3-stop gray ramp from 0.30 up to 0.55, versus m02's neutral bright
silver or m03's violet-tinted titanium) since scored steel reads rougher
than a brushed finish.

**Roughness is intentionally flat, not relief-driven.** Unlike a
relief-proportional roughness ramp, roughness here is a flat texture
(`rough_const`, value 0.6) fed through a dedicated node rather than left
as a Material-node scalar only -- the standing lesson every from-scratch
cookbook material follows so an ORM map actually exports. The reasoning
for keeping it flat rather than scratch-driven: scored steel's
matte-vs-glossy read is fairly uniform across the surface, the variation
is in scratch angle and position, not local roughness.

`metallic=1.0` scalar throughout (uniform metal, same reasoning as
`m02`/`m03`: there is no paint layer to mask off). `normal_map`'s
`param4=0` is the standing flat-normal fix for a directly-fed analytic
generator.

## Subgraph structure

Two named subgraphs, the same convention every cookbook recipe follows:

- **Scratch Finish** -- the retyped `scratches` generator and its albedo
  colorize. Exposed: `Steel color` (the gradient), `Scratch randomness`.
- **Surface Detail** -- the normal map and the flat roughness texture.
  Exposed: `Roughness`, `Scratch depth` (the normal map's relief
  strength).

The generator lives in the color group rather than staying top-level,
since it feeds all three downstream nodes (albedo colorize, normal map,
roughness texture) and folding it into one group avoids a degenerate
single-node "finish" group -- the same move `t09_rippled_wet_sand` and
every other from-scratch cookbook material makes.

## Honest limitation

The 3D preview scene renders metals dark (a project-wide, scene-level
caveat, not specific to this material): the dark environment reflects
poorly off metallic surfaces, so the scratch pattern reads mostly as
faint pale lines against a near-black surface in the composite preview
rather than a bright brushed-steel look. Judge this material by the
scratch pattern and relief, or read the exported ORM/normal channels
directly, rather than by overall brightness in the preview.
