# w06_burled_wood - Burled walnut, swirling figure

_Category: wood. Open the graph: `cookbook/wood/w06_burled_wood.ptex`._

Rich walnut-burl surface: broad swirling, knotted grain figure instead of
straight parallel grain, achieved by retyping one of the donor's two
chained `warp` nodes to `warp2`.

## Recipe

Clones the same `wood` donor as `w04_driftwood_gray` and `w05_dark_walnut`,
but goes past a pure recolor: the donor's `wood_grain` chain contains two
chained `warp` nodes (`GrainWarp`, then a second warp originally distorted
by a thresholded `voronoi` ring pattern for a growth-ring/cathedral
figure). This material retypes that second warp node to `warp2`
(`BurlSwirl`) and replaces its displacement input.

The donor's `voronoi_0` -> `colorize_1` ring-pattern chain (the original
displacement source) is removed outright rather than folded into a
subgraph as dead code, unlike `w03_painted_wood_siding`'s inherited
`combine_0` (that was dead code that came WITH the `wooden_floor` donor).
Here the dead code would be introduced BY this edit itself, since nothing
else in this material still needs the ring pattern once its consumer is
repointed, so deleting it is more honest than leaving an orphaned generator
sitting in the graph.

In its place, a new low-frequency `perlin` node (`SwirlField`, `scale_x=2,
scale_y=2, iterations=3, persistence=0.5`, roughly isotropic and about an
order of magnitude coarser than `GrainNoiseFine`'s `scale_x=32`) feeds
`BurlSwirl`'s displacement input, producing broad, roughly circular swirls
instead of voronoi's cell-boundary rings.

`BurlSwirl`'s `amount` is pushed from `debug_swatches.py`'s
`build_swatch_warp2`'s verified 0.3 starting point up to 0.65 (`warp2`'s
`amount` range is 0..1 per `describe_node`, unlike `warp`'s unbounded
`amount`), high enough that the swirl clearly dominates the underlying
straight grain and produces distinct concentric burl "eyes," short of the
point where the field folds into illegible noise.

Palette: richer contrast than `w05_dark_walnut`, with a near-black "burl
eye" low point and a warm honey-brown high point so the swirl pattern
itself reads as figure, not just a recolor. Same semi-gloss roughness ramp
as `w05_dark_walnut` (a finished/sealed furniture surface, not raw timber).

**`warp2` is a Transform-category node, not a noise/pattern generator** --
per Material Maker's own `tree_item` taxonomy (`"warp" -> "Transform/Warp"`),
it belongs to `docs/AUTHORING.md`'s separate "Distortion vocabulary"
section, not the "Noise vocabulary" one. This material therefore does not
move `quality/node_usage_audit.py`'s noise/pattern coverage numbers, unlike
every other material added this round. A future reader checking the audit
script for `warp2` usage should not expect to find it counted there.

## Subgraph structure

Grouped per the "Grouping into subgraphs" lever in `docs/AUTHORING.md`,
same split as `w04_driftwood_gray`/`w05_dark_walnut`:

- **Wood Grain** -- the noise/pattern generator chain (the three original
  grain perlins, the new low-frequency `SwirlField` perlin, the warp pair
  with the second warp now `warp2`, and the blend that produces the grain
  mask) plus the albedo colorize that paints its output. Exposed: `Wood
  color` (the walnut-burl albedo gradient).
- **Surface Finish** -- the roughness ramp and the normal map. Exposed:
  `Finish sheen` (the semi-gloss roughness gradient).
