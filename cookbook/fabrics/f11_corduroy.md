# f11_corduroy - Corduroy

_Category: fabrics. Open the graph: `cookbook/fabrics/f11_corduroy.ptex`._

A warm tan corduroy fabric with dark umber shadow in the rib grooves and
a lighter tan highlight on the rib crests. The first cookbook material
to use `directional_noise`, a compound node with three internal
composite sub-networks selectable via a mode switch.

## Recipe

Built from scratch via `_from_scratch_noise_material` (no donor has this
topology), then the placeholder generator is `retype()`d to
`directional_noise` at its own defaults: `param0=0` ("Noise 1"),
`n_scale=1`, `param1=11` (read from `directional_noise.mmg`'s own
`gen_parameters` block). Both node types expose a plain `f` scalar on
output port 0, so the swap is connection-safe. `n_scale` (range 1-8) is
exposed as the rib-density knob: it scales every internal fbm2/perlin/
tiler branch inside "Noise 1" together, so raising it tightens the ribs
without touching any internal sub-network directly.

**A real, disclosed limitation, found by actually comparing all three
internal modes rather than trusting the first one.** `directional_noise`
has three internal sub-networks ("Noise 1"/"Noise 2"/"Noise 3", picked
by `param0`). An isolated verification render of all three, followed by
a measured band-regularity comparison (coefficient of variation of both
gap spacing and band width, computed from each render's actual pixels),
found:

- `param0=0` ("Noise 1", the one shipped here): anisotropic horizontal
  streaking, genuinely IRREGULAR -- variable band width, wandering
  lines, uneven spacing. Not clean, evenly-spaced parallel ribbing.
- `param0=1` ("Noise 2"): also anisotropic streaking, measurably LESS
  regular than mode 0 (worse coefficient of variation on both axes).
- `param0=2` ("Noise 3"): smooth, blurry, widely-spaced soft waves with
  no fine grain at all -- not a repeating rib pattern, and the worst
  match of the three.

`param0=0` was kept as the best of three real options, not because it
matches an idealized "clean parallel ribbing" description -- it is the
most regular of the two modes that show genuine repeating anisotropic
banding, and it is a structurally different family from the
cellular/blotchy `fbm`-based looks used elsewhere in this category
(the actual goal), even though it does not read as tight, evenly-spaced
corduroy wales the way a literal photo reference would.

Palette is a real 3-stop ramp (dark umber groove, warm tan base, light
tan highlight) so the ribbing itself carries the color variation rather
than a flat tint. Soft matte roughness (0.85) fed as a flat texture via
`rough_const` so an ORM map exports. `normal_map`'s `param4=0` is the
standing flat-normal fix; `param1=0.55` (relief strength) is stronger
than `f09_plaid_flannel`'s 0.42 nap and `f04_wool_knit`'s 0.3 soft ribs,
since corduroy wales are meant to be a real, pronounced physical ridge.

## Subgraph structure

Two named subgraphs:

- **Corduroy Rib** -- the retyped `directional_noise` generator and its
  albedo colorize. Exposed: `Rib density` (`n_scale`), `Corduroy color`.
- **Corduroy Finish** -- the normal map and the flat roughness texture.
  Exposed: `Roughness`, `Relief strength`.

## Honest limitation

This material's underlying noise field is genuinely more irregular than
real corduroy's evenly-spaced wales -- it was chosen as the best of the
node's three available internal modes, not because any of them nails a
photo-real corduroy read. If a future session finds a way to regularize
the banding further (e.g. by feeding the field through an additional
tiling/threshold pass), that would be a real improvement over this
recipe, not a correction of a claim made here.
